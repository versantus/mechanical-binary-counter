What is this?

A binary counter made of wood, with no electronics. Each of the eight blocks is one binary digit, worth 1, 2, 4 and so on up to 128, reading from the right. Add up the values of the blocks showing 1 and you have the number. Push the right-hand block to add one. If it was already a 1, it turns back to 0 and its gate carries the next block over, just as you carry a 1 when adding on paper. Eight blocks count from 0 to 255. Click any other block to flip it by hand, and drag or scroll to look around.

The blocks

Eight flat pine blocks, cut like dice, each with its place value (1 to 128) burnt into the top and bottom edges, hang on a common dowel between two end posts. Each has a pokerwork 0 on the front and a 1 on the back, and every push turns a block half a turn over the top. Every 1 face except the leftmost carries a small walnut gate on a pin.

The gate

With the 1 at the back, the gate's pin is near the bottom, so gravity lets it hang down along the face, out of the way. Turn the block over and the pin is near the top: the gate stands up, topples left onto its stop peg and locks across the front of the next block.

The carry

Push a 1 and, as it turns over, the locked gate sweeps up over the top edge of the next block and takes it round too. If that block was also a 1, its own gate does the same to the next, which is why 0111 1111 to 1000 0000 ripples along the whole row.

Counting

Eight bits count from 0 to 255. One more push turns every block back to 0 and the carry falls off the left end, which is overflow you can watch happen.

Build your own

These steps make the counter in the model: eight pine blocks on a dowel, each with a walnut gate that drops into place when the block shows 1. The finished piece is 431 mm long, 127 mm tall and 75 mm deep. Allow a weekend. All sizes are in millimetres and come from the model, so drill one test block and check the gate works before doing all seven.

Steps

    If something sticks

    • A gate doesn't drop. It's binding on its pin. Open the hole slightly or ease the washer. Check the lean peg leaves it leaning left.
    • The carry slips off. Not enough gate reaches the next block. Move the pin 1 to 2 mm further left or make the gate longer.
    • One block drags its neighbour. Too much friction between them. Wax the faces and use a thinner washer.
    • Blocks swing past 0 or 1. The axle hole is off-centre or too loose. A slightly tighter washer stack adds friction.

    Finishing

    Sand to 240, then use Danish oil or clear wax. Keep finish off the axle holes, the pins and the faces the gates slide over, or the action gets sticky. Wax the axle once the finish is dry.
